{{< lead >}}
Do regions in Rio Grande do Sul function around a single dominant city or around several centers, and what does that mean for regional development?
{{< /lead >}}

{{< facts >}}

## The challenge

**Polycentrism**, a territory organized around several complementary centers rather than one, is a goal of regional planning in Europe and increasingly in Brazil. But administrative regions rarely match how people actually live and move. The project asked how polycentric the regions of Rio Grande do Sul really are, starting from their medium-sized cities.

## Approach

This was my main formative research experience: I joined as an undergraduate research assistant and stayed through my PhD. Across four planning regions, we combined official urban hierarchy data with commuting flows to delineate **functional urban areas (FUAs)**.

## Results

- Functional urban areas identified around **Santa Cruz do Sul and Lajeado** (Region 2), **Ijuí, Santa Rosa and Santo Ângelo** (Region 7), **Santa Maria** (Region 8), and **Passo Fundo, Erechim, Carazinho and Frederico Westphalen** (Region 9).
- An initial process of FUA formation, driven by commuting for work and study, with medium-sized cities as the anchors.
- A large body of work: two books, around ten articles and several chapters, forming a theoretical and methodological basis for later research and for public policy.

## Why it matters

Functional areas show where integrated planning, from public transport to health and education services, makes more sense than municipal boundaries. The method relies on open data and can be replicated in other regions.
